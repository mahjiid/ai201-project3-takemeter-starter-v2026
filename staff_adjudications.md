## Pattern diagnosis — draft for review

The 13 differences follow recurring mistakes: (1) labeling a data-backed comparison as a reaction or hot take (sp_004, sp_008, sp_009); (2) treating a bare numeric threshold or striking metaphor as evidence (sp_012, sp_016, sp_018); (3) confusing general claims with immediate feelings (sp_011, sp_020); and (4) misreading first-person, recently felt experiences as generic assertions or analysis (sp_023, sp_025, sp_027, sp_028). sp_003 is a genuine plausible disagreement about whether the available checkable facts do enough work to support the particular causal claim. These findings suggest **annotation-boundary application is a risk**, but do not prove this is the cause of the separate four-label basketball model's failures.

## What this evidence establishes

The explicit judgments above support a 12-to-1 pattern in this analysis: twelve staff interpretations are more persuasive on the stated rules, while one initial `hot_take` choice (sp_003) remains defensible. This is an **adjudication pattern**, not a recalculated agreement score; the agreement remains **17/30 = 56.7%** because the original blind annotations are immutable.

**Diagnosis:** Some error comes from inconsistent application of load-bearing facts versus illustrative numbers and from confusing emotionally phrased generalizations with immediate personal reports. The original four-label basketball classifier is a different domain and taxonomy; agreement on running-forum posts demonstrates labeling-risk awareness but cannot prove the cause of the classifier's failure to learn `reaction`.
